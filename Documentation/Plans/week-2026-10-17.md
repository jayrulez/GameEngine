## Editor.Scene's pages move to their domains (2026-10-18)

From the 0.1 backlog (user 2026-10-16): Editor.Scene held the pages of other domains beside the
scene page, and play in editor too. Each now lives in its domain's editor library, as the audio,
font, texture and shader pages already did, and builds and is tested there:

| Library | Module | What moved | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| Editor.Animation | `editor.animation` | the animation graph, clip and skeleton pages | Editor.Animation.Tests |
| Editor.Materials | `editor.materials` | the material page | Editor.Materials.Tests |
| Editor.Geometry | `editor.geometry` | the mesh page (static, skinned and model meshes) | Editor.Geometry.Tests |
| Editor.Particles | `editor.particles` | the particle effect page | Editor.Particles.Tests |
| Editor.Render | `editor.render` | the settings profile page | Editor.Render.Tests |
| Editor.Play | `editor.play` | play in editor: the Game tab, its resolutions, IPieInstancePage, the PIE tools, the game-audio settings | Editor.Play.Tests |

**Where play in editor lives** (the item asked for a decision): in Editor.Play, a library of its
own, since it runs a project rather than editing a scene or an asset. It builds on Editor.Scene
(a Game tab captures its viewport as a scene page does) and plugs in through the context's
existing `GamePageFactory`; `RegisterPlayInEditor` sets that and contributes the PIE tools, so
Editor.Scene no longer depends on play in editor and `RegisterSceneEditor` no longer takes the
embedded application. entity_inspect's `pie` reads a running game through a narrower service the
scene module owns, `IGameScenePage` (the tab's id, whether it runs, its scene), which
IPieInstancePage builds on; the editor's MCP tools are unchanged.

Editor.Scene keeps what scenes share: the settings rows (the profile page uses them, so
Editor.Render builds on Editor.Scene as Editor.Play does), the profile model, loading and framing
a project scene, the viewport capture, and the asset thumbnails, which share one preview stage
(the mesh, material, skeleton, model and particle thumbnails stay with it). The other new
libraries need nothing of Editor.Scene. `PrettifyPropertyName`, which the inspector and the
material page share, moved to Editor.App beside `ResourceRefEditor`. The moved pages now import
what they name (the fonts, the settings store) instead of reaching it through `editor.scene`.
